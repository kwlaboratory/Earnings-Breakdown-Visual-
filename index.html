Variant Perception

Bull vs. Bear After the Print

The quarter was unambiguously strong, but the debate shifts to: is Q2 a new earnings run-rate or a peak aided by episodic catalysts (SpaceX-adjacent IPOs, M&A completions, tariff volatility driving FICC)? Smart people disagree.

Bull Case
  • IB backlog growing QoQ and vs. YE 2025 — not a one-quarter event; pipeline is deepening
  • Equities structural shift — prime brokerage + derivatives are durable annuity-like revenues tied to rising hedge fund AUM globally
  • AUS now $4T — management fee base growing 20% YoY creates a recurring earnings floor regardless of market cycles
  • Consumer drag is nearly over — Platform Solutions shrinks to irrelevance; provisions collapse 73% YoY
  • Capital return accelerating — dividend raised 11%, $4B buyback in a single quarter; buyback yield ~6% annualized
  • Asia up 102% YoY — geographic diversification adds incremental top-line; Asia IPO and wealth markets still underpenetrated
Bear / Risk Case
  • Equities record may not repeat — $7.4B is 72% above last year; derivatives/prime gains partly episodic; IPO cycle is lumpy
  • Valuation re-rated significantly — stock near $1,130 implies 17–18x forward EPS; stretched vs. historical mean of ~12x book earnings
  • VaR creeping higher — avg daily VaR $120M vs. $112M Q1; if volatility drops and positioning normalizes, trading revenues compress sharply
  • NIM headwind in AWM — Private banking revenues down 13% YoY as Marcus deposit NIM compresses; rate cuts would amplify this
  • Efficiency ratio leverage limited — comp ratio moves with revenue; a $3–4B revenue reversal would flip the operating leverage sharply
  • Oppenheimer downgrade pre-earnings (Underperform) cited exactly this: best-case scenarios already priced; risk/reward asymmetric

Quick Glossary

Key terms used throughout this primer — explained for context, not jargon.

IB Fees Backlog
Advisory and underwriting transactions that are announced but not yet closed. Closed = booked revenue. Rising backlog = forward visibility into future quarters.
FICC
Fixed Income, Currencies & Commodities. GS market-makes in these instruments for clients, earning bid-ask spreads plus financing revenue from secured lending.
Equities Intermediation
Market-making and derivatives in equities — profiting from facilitating client trades. Highly correlated to volatility and trading volumes.
Equities Financing
Prime brokerage — lending cash and securities to hedge funds. Revenue from interest income and fees on securities lending. Stickier than intermediation.
AUS
Assets Under Supervision — total client assets managed or advised by GS AWM. Drives management fee revenues (typically 30–60bps annually on long-term assets).
CET1 Ratio
Common Equity Tier 1 — highest quality regulatory capital as % of risk-weighted assets. Higher = more buffer above minimums = more capacity for buybacks/dividends.
ROE
Return on (Common) Equity. GS's target is 15–17% through the cycle. 23.5% in Q2 2026 is well above target — investors question sustainability at this level.
GCLA
Global Core Liquid Assets — unencumbered cash and highly liquid securities. GS's liquidity war chest. $555B avg in Q2 2026 provides substantial stress buffer.
Efficiency Ratio
Total expenses / Total revenues. Lower = more operationally efficient. 58.8% H1 2026 vs. 62% H1 2025 shows positive operating leverage as revenues grew faster than costs.
VaR
Value at Risk — statistical measure of potential daily loss at 95% confidence. GS uses it to size trading risk. Higher VaR ≠ crisis, but indicates elevated market exposure.
HFS (Held for Sale)
Assets classified as held for sale are carried at lower of cost or fair value. Apple Card portfolio in HFS triggered markdowns in Platform Solutions revenues.
P/B Ratio
Price-to-Book. For banks, compares market cap to tangible book value. A P/B above 1x signals the market expects ROE above cost of equity. GS trades ~3x P/B at current prices.
